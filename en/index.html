About the project

Where mathematics meets storytelling

The Chronicles of Ruthenia is a unique project in which the Godot game engine (desktop version) handles all the math: dice rolls, damage calculation, hit chances, skill and survival checks. Meanwhile an AI Narrator (a local llama model or a cloud LLM) acts as an endlessly creative Game Master, describing the world, dialogue and consequences of your actions. The browser demo on this page is built with Phaser 3.

But most importantly — strict historical authenticity. A RAG knowledge base compiled from hundreds of historical books and articles keeps the neural network from hallucinating and inventing facts. Everything that happens in the game is checked for historical accuracy.

📜

RAG Historical Knowledge Base

A multi-source Retrieval-Augmented Generation system filled with real historical sources on 15th-century Rus' and world history. The AI does not invent — it selects from verified facts.

🧮

BRP Universal Game Engine d100

The mathematically rigorous Basic Roleplaying system, carefully adapted to the 15th-century Rus' setting, with realistic odds, skills and combat mechanics.

🤖

LLM Narrator

A local neural network on llama.cpp server, or any cloud model via an OpenAI-compatible API. Narrative quality depends on the chosen model and its weights.

⚔️

Realistic Turn-Based Combat

Graphical combat in 2.5D isometric view or a text mode. Historically accurate weapons, armour and clothing. You can die from a single well-placed blow to a vulnerable spot.

🌍

A Living Historical World

Every world event is reflected in the game: rumours from merchants, news from foreign guests, political shifts. Day and night, seasons and weather change. Realistic roads and distances.

💾

Smart Memory Management

Rolling Summary + State Injection around the model's context window. No context overflow — the AI always remembers the key events of your story.

A chronicle of events

Chronicle 1400–1505

Key events of the era — from Edigu's invasion to the end of Ivan III's reign. The “Quest for the Lost Icon” scenario is woven into this chronicle: rumours of great battles reach the village with the trade caravans.

1408
Edigu's invasion
The Horde emir Edigu ravages the northern lands but fails to take Moscow.
1410
Battle of Grunwald
Poland and Lithuania crush the Teutonic Order. News of the battle reaches Rus'.
1425
Feudal war begins
Vasily I dies — Vasily II and Yuri of Zvenigorod dispute the grand princely throne.
1440
Ivan III is born
The future “Sovereign of All Rus'” is born in the year the Byzantine emperors fall.
1448
Autocephaly of the Russian Church
A council of Russian bishops installs Metropolitan Jonas without Constantinople.
1453
Fall of Constantinople
Byzantium perishes under the Ottoman onslaught. Rus' sees itself as the last Orthodox power.
1462
Ivan III takes the throne
The beginning of a 43-year reign that would redraw the map of Eastern Europe.
1471
Battle of Shelon
Moscow defeats Novgorod. The independence of the free city ends.
1480
Stand on the Ugra
The end of the Horde yoke — 240 years of dependence end without a battle.
1497
Ivan III's Law Code (Sudebnik)
The first unified law code of the Russian state. St George's Day for peasants.
1505
End of an era
Ivan III dies. A state grown threefold passes to Vasily III.

Historical context

15th-century Rus' — an age of change

The age of “gathering of the Russian lands” around the Principality of Moscow, of feudal wars and of casting off the Horde yoke. Three grand princes define the epoch.

1400–1425
Vasily I Dmitriyevich
Grand Prince of Moscow. Consolidation of the principality, struggle with Lithuania and the Horde.
1425–1462
Vasily II “the Blind”
The era of feudal wars, the prince's blinding, and the struggle for the Moscow throne.
1462–1505
Ivan III “the Great”
Overthrow of the Horde yoke (1480), unification of the Russian lands, construction of the Kremlin.

The game accounts for real historical events of 15th-century Rus' and all major world events. Rumours from merchants, tales of foreign guests, political changes — all of it is reflected in the game world, and none of it can be missed.

Technical foundation

How it works

🏗️ Project architecture

  • Godot game engine — manages the game environment, loads neural networks, performs all calculations and supervises the historical accuracy of prompts via the RAG base.
  • Local neural network — downloaded from a Hugging Face repository on first launch. Works offline through llama.cpp server.
  • Cloud models — connect any model with your OpenAI-compatible API key by specifying the provider address and model name.
  • RAG knowledge base — compiled from several hundred historical books and articles. Verifies every prompt for historical accuracy before it reaches the neural network.
  • BRP d100 — the Chaosium BRP Universal Game Engine SRD roleplaying system. All rolls, damage, skills and checks are computed by the engine.
  • Built-in embedding model — mxbai-embed-large-v1 · Q5_K_M · dim 1024 · cosine search. A second neural network for the RAG knowledge base.
  • Rolling Summary + State Injection — intelligent context-window management that prevents overflow while preserving key events.
  • Full list of neural network models:
    • Local: Saiga Llama 3 8B (Q2_K/Q4_K_M/Q8_0), Saiga Gemma 3 12B (Q3_K_M/Q4_K_M/Q6), Vikhr-Nemo 12B (Q4_K_M/Q5_K_M/Q6), Vikhr-YandexGPT 5 Lite 8B (Q4_K_M), Gemma-4 E4B-Uncensored (Q4_K_M)
    • Cloud: Any cloud neural network can be connected using an OpenAI-compatible API key.
    • Embedder (knowledge base): mxbai-embed-large-v1 · Q5_K_M · dim 1024 · cosine search
